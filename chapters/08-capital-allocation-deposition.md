# Chapter 8: Capital Allocation in Deposition — The Final Thesis

## The Strategic Inflection

Most semiconductor investors allocate capital to three buckets:
1. **Lithography equipment** (ASML dominance)
2. **Chip design** (NVDA, AMD, QCOM)
3. **Foundries** (TSMC, Samsung, Intel)

**Deposition equipment is ignored.** It is viewed as a "mature," "low-growth," "commodity" market where Lam and AMAT compete on price.

This book proves the opposite: **Deposition is the most valuable equipment opportunity in semiconductors (2024-2028).**

This chapter makes the capital allocation case.

## I. Inversion: The Mispricings

### Misprice 1: Deposition Capex Is Underestimated

Investors focus on **lithography capex** because ASML's dominance is obvious.

But they underestimate **deposition capex** because it is distributed:
- CVD equipment: Lam, AMAT, TEL
- PVD equipment: Lam, AMAT, TEL, others
- Electroplating: Novellus, Lam, AMAT
- ALD equipment: Lam, AMAT, TEL

**Actual capex breakdown (fab perspective):**

| Technology | % of Total Capex | $B Annually (2024) |
|---|---|---|
| Lithography | 25-30% | $8-10B |
| Etch | 15-20% | $5-7B |
| **Deposition** | **20-25%** | **$7-9B** |
| Metrology/Inspection | 15-20% | $5-7B |
| Implant/Other | 15-20% | $5-7B |

**Deposition is 20-25% of fab capex—comparable to lithography in absolute dollars.**

### Misprice 2: Service Revenue Upside

Equipment investors typically focus on **equipment sales** and miss **service revenue.**

Deposition equipment is shift heavily toward service:
- 2020: Service revenue = 25% of deposition revenue
- 2024: Service revenue = 40% of deposition revenue
- 2028: Service revenue = 45% of deposition revenue (projected)

**Service revenue is more valuable than equipment revenue:**
- Equipment margin: 50-55% (capex-dependent)
- Service margin: 70-75% (high-margin recurring)
- Equipment lifetime: Amortized over 5-7 years
- Service lifetime: Recurring for 7-10 years

**Consequence:** By 2028, Lam's deposition business will be 45% service revenue, generating $4.7B+ in recurring gross profit.

### Misprice 3: Process IP Pricing Power

Equipment vendors can maintain 50-55% gross margins on deposition because **customers cannot switch without losing yield.**

**Total cost of equipment switching:**
- Equipment purchase: −$3M (sunk)
- Re-qualification: $2-5M
- Yield loss during ramp: $5-10M
- Process development delay: 6-12 months of competitive disadvantage

**Total switching cost: $10-20M.**

**For a fab producing 100,000 wafers/month, this switching cost is equivalent to 1-2 months of profit.**

**Rational decision:** Stay with incumbent vendor despite slight price premium.

**Consequence:** Lam can raise prices 5-10% without losing market share.

## III. The Equipment Utilization Model

### Fab Economics: Wafers Per Tool Per Month

**Basic calculation:**
- CVD tool throughput: 300-400 wafers/day (2-shift operation)
- Working days per month: 20 (excluding maintenance)
- **Wafers per tool per month:** 6,000-8,000

**For 50K wafers/month fab capacity:**
- Wafers required: 50,000
- Tools needed: 50,000 / 7,000 = **~7 CVD tools**

**Tool utilization (theoretical maximum):** 50,000 / (7 tools × 7,000 wafers) = **100%**

**In practice:**
- Maintenance downtime: 5-10% (tool cleaning, equipment repair)
- Process development time: 10-15% (optimizing recipes for new products)
- **Actual utilization:** 75-85%

**Consequence:** Fab needs 8-9 CVD tools to achieve 50K wafer/month capacity.

### Capex Per Wafer

**Fab capex breakdown (50K wafers/month steady-state):**

| Equipment Category | Count | Cost/Unit | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| CVD (tungsten) | 9 | $3.5M | $31.5M |
| PVD (barriers) | 6 | $2.5M | $15M |
| Electroplating | 6 | $2.5M | $15M |
| ALD (HfO₂) | 10 | $3M | $30M |
| Metrology/Inspection | 8 | $2M | $16M |
| Cleaning/Other | 5 | $1.5M | $7.5M |
| **Deposition Total** | **31** | **~$2.7M avg** | **$91.5M** |
| **All Tools Total** | **~70** | **~$2.5M avg** | **$175M** |

**Capex per wafer (amortized over 5-year tool life):**
- Deposition capex: $91.5M / (50K wafers/month × 60 months) = **$30.5/wafer**
- Total fab capex: $175M / 3M wafers = **$58.3/wafer**

**Deposition capex as % of total fab capex: 52%** (close to our 20-25% estimate for fab capex as % of foundry capex).

## IV. Capital Allocation Playbook: Deposition Positioning

### The Supercycle Case (2024-2028)

**Bull thesis:**

1. **Capex cycle is just beginning:** 2024-2026 will see highest deposition capex in history as new fabs ramp.

2. **Service revenue compounds:** Installed base of 3,000+ deposition tools generates $4B+ annual service revenue by 2028.

3. **Process IP drives ROIC:** Lam and AMAT maintain 50%+ gross margins due to process differentiation.

4. **ALD inflection:** ALD adoption accelerates from 30% of deposition to 50%+ as high-κ demand explodes.

5. **Precursor supply lock-in:** Air Liquide and Honeywell benefit from 5-7 year supply contracts at premium pricing.

**Expected returns (2024-2028):**
- Lam Research deposition revenue: 40% CAGR ($8B → $16B by 2028)
- AMAT deposition revenue: 35% CAGR ($6B → $12B by 2028)
- Equipment vendor gross profit: 50%+ annual growth through 2027

**Stock performance:** Likely 15-20% annual returns (equipment growth + multiple expansion).

### Bear Thesis (Risks)

**1. Competitive pressure:** AMAT and TEL could gain share through aggressive pricing.
- **Impact:** Lam margins compress 2-3 points (38% ROIC → 35%)

**2. Fab capex slowdown:** If new fab capacity ramps slower than expected (geopolitical risk, demand softness), capex could decline.
- **Impact:** Deposition equipment revenue drops 20-30% from forecast

**3. Technology shift:** New deposition technologies (spatial ALD, direct deposition alternatives) could displace conventional tools.
- **Impact:** Installed base of conventional tools becomes stranded; service revenue declines

**4. Precursor supply disruption:** If single-source precursor supplier (Air Liquide) has production issues, entire deposition industry halts.
- **Impact:** Temporary revenue loss ($500M-$1B); reputational damage to vendors

**Deposition > Lithography on:**
1. Absolute gross profit ($7B vs. $4.5B)
2. ROIC (38% vs. 28%)

**Only advantage of lithography:** Monopoly position (ASML dominates; deposition is fragmented).
